  <h1>What is GLP-1?</h1>
  <p>GLP-1 (Glucagon-Like Peptide-1) is a natural hormone your gut releases after eating. It helps regulate blood sugar and appetite. My GLP Shot is a tracking app built specifically for people using GLP-1 medications—like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound—to manage their dose, side effects, and progress.</p>

  <p class="muted small">Last updated: <time datetime="2026-05-05">May 5, 2026</time>. This page provides a plain-language overview of GLP-1 for users of our tracker. It is not medical advice. For treatment decisions, consult your doctor.</p>

  <h2>The basics: your body's GLP-1</h2>
  <p>Your body makes GLP-1 in the intestines after a meal. It has several key jobs:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Signals insulin release:</strong> Tells the pancreas to produce insulin, which lowers blood sugar.</li>
    <li><strong>Reduces glucagon:</strong> Tells the liver to stop releasing stored glucose into the bloodstream.</li>
    <li><strong>Slows stomach emptying:</strong> Food leaves your stomach more slowly, which helps you feel full longer.</li>
    <li><strong>Sends fullness signals to the brain:</strong> Acts on appetite centers in the brain to reduce hunger.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>In people with type 2 diabetes or obesity, the body's natural GLP-1 system may not work as effectively. GLP-1 medications are designed to augment or mimic this hormone.</p>

  <h2>GLP-1 medications: how they work</h2>
  <p>GLP-1 receptor agonists are synthetic versions of the hormone that are resistant to being broken down quickly. When injected, they activate the same GLP-1 receptors throughout the body, producing stronger and longer-lasting effects than the natural hormone.</p>
  <p>These effects lead to the benefits seen with medications like semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound): improved blood sugar control, reduced appetite, and weight loss.</p>
  <p>Tracking these medications is where a purpose-built tool like <a href="/">My GLP Shot</a> comes in. Because the medication has a specific half-life in your body, its concentration rises and falls over days. Understanding this cycle helps you correlate it with your appetite, side effects, and weight changes.</p>

  <div class="faq-section">
    <h3>What does GLP-1 stand for?</h3>
    <p>GLP-1 stands for Glucagon-Like Peptide-1. It is a hormone your body naturally produces in the gut after eating. Its main jobs are to signal the pancreas to release insulin, tell the liver to stop releasing excess glucose, slow down stomach emptying, and send fullness signals to the brain.</p>

    <h3>Are GLP-1 medications the same as insulin?</h3>
    <p>No. Insulin is a hormone that directly lowers blood sugar by helping cells absorb glucose. GLP-1 medications are GLP-1 receptor agonists. They mimic the natural GLP-1 hormone, which has several effects: increasing insulin release (only when blood sugar is high), reducing glucagon (which tells the liver to release glucose), slowing digestion, and increasing feelings of fullness. They are used for both type 2 diabetes and weight loss.</p>

    <h3>What are the most common GLP-1 medications?</h3>
    <p>Common GLP-1 medications include semaglutide (brand names Ozempic for diabetes, Wegovy for weight loss), tirzepatide (Mounjaro for diabetes, Zepbound for weight loss), and liraglutide (Saxenda for weight loss, Victoza for diabetes). These are typically administered via a weekly or daily injection.</p>
